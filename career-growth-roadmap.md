## PATH 1 — Staff Engineer → Principal Engineer → Distinguished Engineer

The **IC (Individual Contributor)** leadership track. You lead through technical excellence, not headcount.

### Level Progression:

| Level | Scope | What You Do |
|---|---|---|
| **Senior Tech Lead (now)** | One team / one product | Owns team's technical decisions, writes/reviews code, mentors |
| **Staff Engineer** | 2-4 teams / a product area | Drives cross-team architecture, identifies problems before they become fires |
| **Principal Engineer** | Entire engineering org (50-200 engineers) | Defines 1-3 year technical strategy, represents engineering in business discussions |
| **Distinguished / Fellow** | Industry-level | Patents, open source leadership, conference keynotes |

---

### Technical Skills to Build NOW:

| Skill | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| **System design at scale** | Design for 10M+ users, not just current scale |
| **Distributed systems** | Consensus (Raft, Paxos), CRDTs, eventual consistency trade-offs |
| **Observability** | OpenTelemetry, distributed tracing, SLOs/SLAs, error budgets |
| **Cloud native** | Kubernetes internals, service mesh (Istio), eBPF |
| **Data engineering** | Flink, Spark, data lakes — most senior engineers underestimate this |
| **Security** | Threat modeling, zero trust — not just "add authentication" |

---

### Non-Technical Skills (This is what separates Staff from Senior):

- **Written communication** — write design docs, RFCs, ADRs that convince people
- **Navigating ambiguity** — define problems, not just solve them
- **Saying no with data** — push back on bad technical decisions with evidence
- **Building consensus** — you can't force decisions at Staff level; you persuade
- **Thinking in systems** — understand how your decision affects teams 3 hops away

## PATH 2 — Engineering Manager → Director → VP Engineering → CTO

The **management track**. You lead through people, process, and culture.

### Mindset Shift Required:

> Right now you measure success by **code quality and architecture**.
> In management, you measure success by **team output, retention, and career growth of your reports**.

### Skills to Build:

- **1:1 mastery** — structured 1:1s that develop people, not just status updates
- **Hiring** — writing job specs, structured interviews, calibration
- **Performance management** — PIPs, promotions, difficult conversations
- **Roadmap negotiation** — pushing back on PMs with data
- **OKRs / goal setting** — cascade org goals to team goals
- **Budget management** — headcount planning, vendor negotiation

### Honest Warning:
Many strong engineers become bad managers because they miss coding and micromanage.
Be sure you **genuinely want this** before committing.

## One Hard Truth

> **10 years of experience is NOT the same as 10 years of growth.**

Many engineers have **1 year of real growth repeated 10 times**. The engineers who advance are the ones who:

1. Deliberately work on their weaknesses — not just doubling down on strengths
2. Seek out problems bigger than their current skill level
3. Communicate their work loudly and clearly — good work that nobody hears about doesn't get you promoted
4. Build a reputation outside their immediate team

## What Separates Senior from Staff/Principal

| Senior Engineer | Staff / Principal Engineer |
|---|---|
| Executes well-defined projects | Defines what projects should exist |
| Solves problems given to them | Finds the problems worth solving |
| Scope = one team | Scope = multiple teams or org |
| Technical decisions for one area | Technical decisions that affect 3+ teams |
| Asks "how do I build this?" | Asks "should we build this at all?" |
| Writes code | Writes design docs, RFCs, strategies |
| Known within the team | Known across the org or industry |

---

## Final Advice

Your **technical foundation is strong**. The gap at your level is almost always:

1. **Communication** — can you write a design doc that convinces skeptics?
2. **Scope** — are you solving problems that matter beyond your team?
3. **Visibility** — do people 2-3 levels above you know your name and your work?

The engineers who become Staff or VP by year 15 stopped asking:
> *"What should I build?"*

And started asking:
> **"What problem should the team be solving — and why?"**
